# Core Game Rules

This document defines the rules shared by every game of Villains & Velvet. Leader Health and Attack, card statistics, abilities, and named effects are content definitions. The deck structure and turn sequence below are core rules. New content may introduce exceptions through card text.

## 1. Objective

Defeat the Enemy Leader by reducing its Health to 0 before the Enemy Deck runs out.

- The player wins immediately when the Enemy Leader reaches 0 Health.
- The Enemy Deck does not reshuffle.
- When the final Enemy card is drawn, finish resolving it and allow the player that turn's Build and Attack phases. If the Leader survives the turn, the player loses.

## 2. Play Areas

The game uses:

- one Enemy Leader area;
- two Minion Areas;
- three Player Build spaces;
- three Player Hand spaces;
- a Player Deck and Player Discard Pile; and
- an Enemy Deck and an Enemy used-card pile.

New Minions enter Area 1. A Minion advances to Area 2 when that space is empty. A Minion in Area 2 escapes only when a Minion in Area 1 pushes it out during a later Advance step.

## 3. Setup

1. Choose an Enemy Leader and set it to that Leader's starting Health.
2. Choose a Scenario.
3. Choose exactly three different Heroes. Add seven Normal, five Ability, and three Special cards for each Hero, then shuffle the 45-card Player Deck.
4. Assemble the 33-card Enemy Deck from the Scenario's 25 Minions and exactly eight Enemy Events selected from the Leader's Leader Strikes and the Scenario's Twists. Shuffle the deck.
5. Leave the Hand, Build, Minion Areas, and discard or used-card piles empty.
6. Begin the first turn.

Content may provide additional setup instructions.

## 4. Turn Sequence

Resolve these seven steps in order each turn.

### Step 1: Draw Player Cards

Draw three cards from the Player Deck. If the deck empties during the draw, recycle the Player Discard Pile and continue drawing if possible.

### Step 2: Advance Minions and Resolve Escapes

1. If both Minion Areas are occupied, the Minion in Area 1 pushes the Area 2 Minion out. Resolve the escaping Minion's Escape effects and any resulting prompts or attacks, then remove it from play.
2. After the Escape finishes, move the Area 1 Minion into Area 2.
3. If Area 2 is occupied and Area 1 is empty, the Area 2 Minion stays in play and does not Escape.
4. If Area 2 is empty, move the Area 1 Minion into Area 2.

A Minion normally gives the player two Attack phases in which to defeat it before it escapes.

### Step 3: Draw Enemy Cards

Draw and resolve Enemy cards until a Minion enters play or the Enemy Deck is empty.

- Resolve a **Leader Strike** immediately, then continue drawing.
- Resolve a **Twist** immediately, then continue drawing.
- Place a **Minion** in Area 1, resolve its Entry effects and attack, and stop drawing. A Minion normally attacks the Build for its Attack value when it enters; its ability may change the order or number of attacks.

If a card specifies an order for its effects, follow its text.

### Step 4: Build

The player may:

- move a Hand card into an empty Build space; or
- swap a Hand card with a card in an occupied Build space.

A card swapped out of the Build moves to the Hand rather than directly to the Discard Pile. Build cards remain between turns unless an effect removes or moves them.

### Step 5: Player Attack

Calculate available Attack from the cards in the Build and their abilities. The player may use that Attack against Minions, the Enemy Leader, or a combination of valid targets.

Minions do not protect the Enemy Leader unless an effect says otherwise. After defeating a Minion, subtract its Health from the available Attack and retain any remainder.

### Step 6: Discard

Move every card remaining in the Hand to the Player Discard Pile. Cards in the Build remain in play.

### Step 7: End Turn

Lose any unused Attack and end the turn. If the Enemy Deck is empty and the Enemy Leader still has Health, the player loses. Otherwise, begin the next turn.

## 5. Attacks and Health

### Cards Use Whole-Card Damage

Player cards and Minions do not retain partial damage. An attack can defeat a card only when its remaining Attack equals or exceeds that card's full Health.

After defeating a card, subtract its Health from the attack. The remainder may defeat another valid card. If no valid card can be defeated, the unused portion of that attack is lost.

Damage to the Enemy Leader reduces its Health normally.

### Multiple Attacks

When an effect makes multiple attacks, resolve each separately. Each begins with the value stated by the effect, and unused Attack does not carry into the next attack.

### Enemy Target Priority

When several Build cards are legal targets, the player chooses which one the enemy attacks. An ability that requires a card to be attacked first limits that choice to cards with that priority.

If no priority card can be defeated, the enemy cannot attack another Build card and loses the rest of that attack.

## 6. Healing and Overflow

An Enemy Leader cannot be healed above its maximum Health. If an effect would exceed that maximum:

1. heal the Leader to its maximum;
2. convert each unused healing point into one point of Overflow Attack; and
3. resolve the Overflow Attack against the Build using the normal enemy-attack rules.

Resolve an escaping Minion's healing and Overflow Attack before removing that Minion from play.

## 7. Entry and Escape Effects

- An Entry effect triggers only when a Minion first enters Area 1.
- Moving a Minion to Area 2 does not repeat its Entry effect.
- An escaping Minion resolves only its Escape effect unless its text says otherwise.
- If an effect requires a target and no valid target exists, that part of the effect does nothing.

## 8. Player Card Zones

- A **destroyed** Build card immediately enters the Player Discard Pile.
- A **discarded** card enters the Player Discard Pile without being destroyed.
- A **swapped** Build card moves to the Hand and is not discarded immediately.
- Build cards are not included when recycling the Player Deck.

When the Player Deck is empty, shuffle the Player Discard Pile to form a new Player Deck and continue any unfinished draw.

## 9. Card Text and Future Content

Card text may add to or override these rules. When card text directly conflicts with a core rule, the card text takes precedence for that effect.

Leaders may define different starting Health, maximum Health, Attack, abilities, and Leader Strikes. Scenarios may provide different Enemy Decks, Twists, and setup rules. Future Hero and Minion sets may introduce new statistics, abilities, and interactions without changing this core document.

Content-specific values and ability explanations belong with the data or documentation for that content set, not in the core rules.
